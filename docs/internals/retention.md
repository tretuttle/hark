# Retention

Retention keeps the background recording inside its body and detail budgets without deleting anything an active export is reading or deleting newer data while an old pack is pinned.

## Running totals, live parts, and open-file rotation

Re-statting every site directory on every flush would add main-thread and I/O cost as the archive grows, so `maintenance.ts` scans disk once at startup after `recover()` and maintains one byte ledger across every writer in the archive root: sealed and open packs, body files, in-flight `incoming/*.part` tees, log segments, and summaries. On startup, `files.ts` marks `ledger.json` active (`clean: false`) with the current `run` before capture begins and trusts a prior `clean: true` ledger only if it parses and matches the archive directories; any unclean, missing, corrupt, or file-inconsistent ledger is treated as unknown and seeded at `maxBodyBytes`. Even when a clean ledger parses, its byte totals are unproven until `loadFromDisk` finishes scanning, so direct pre-scan writes are bounded to a single `FLUSH_BATCH_BYTES` batch (`2 MiB`) for streams with known `Content-Length` fitting inside that batch while larger or lengthless `file-tee` streams buffer in memory (suspending channels at `4 MiB` per stream or `16 MiB` total). On a `30,000 ms` scan timeout with zero proved headroom, suspended channels resume without re-suspending or creating `incoming/*.part` files, keeping bodies that finish inside the RAM buffer and stopping overflows with `"scan-timeout"` rather than mislabeling them `"first-part"`.

Retention runs inline inside the write-chain step whenever a running total crosses `maxBodyBytes` or `maxDetailBytes`, after each segment seal, on pin release, and every ten minutes (`RETENTION_EVERY_MS`). To keep both per-chunk eviction and per-flush `retain()` logarithmic in evicted count (`O(k log N)` instead of scanning or sorting the full inventory), `maintenance.ts` keeps both tracked bodies and tracked details in indexed min-heaps ordered oldest-first alongside an incrementally maintained locked-bytes sum behind the oldest active pin. When a growing `file-tee` part pushes `bodyBytes` past `maxBodyBytes`, `updatePart` schedules `retain()` on the store's write chain (with `partBytes` and open async writes accounted against the cap so all file deletions stay serialized on one chain), and `retain()` schedules async rotation if an active pack is oldest while subtracting open part bytes from `maxBodyBytes`. On each chunk (`onDataAvailable`), if counting pinned data, unfreeable files behind a pin, and other live parts leaves less than the route cap, the tee closes at that remaining budget (`check: "first-part"`, reported on export as `over-body-cap` with its first part kept). Moving a finished part into `bodies/` transfers its ledger entry atomically.

When retention meets an open file while over cap, it emits `rotate` and stops that pass. Because a site may have pending streams in memory referencing its open pack, `store.ts` detaches and flushes those pending streams synchronously onto the write chain while advancing `packSeq` before marking the old pack closed, then loops retention until the rotated file is weighed and deleted.

## Pins and exclusion ordering

- **Pins are live-session only.** `pin()` refcounts each pinned pack, body file, or segment in memory for an in-flight export and does not persist across a restart; recovery on startup and the first retention pass reclaim unpinned over-cap files normally.
- **Pins stop the walk.** While `blocked-by-pin` stops deletion, `getRecordingStatus` reports `over-cap-while-exporting`, and newer files remain untouched. Releasing the last pin triggers retention and clears the problem.
- **Cut site directories.** Sites longer than 64 characters hash to a truncated `SiteDir` that `parseSiteDir` cannot invert, so `maintenance.ts` keeps each tracked entry's exact scanned path on disk rather than reconstructing paths from `RetentionRef.site`.
- **Exclusion while streaming.** Parts in `incoming/` sit outside `sites/<siteDir>/`. An exclusion deletes the site directory immediately, clears `ActivityIndex`, and rejects any late `file-tee` commit after `IOUtils.move` (untracking and deleting its `.part`/`.body`) so an excluded site cannot reappear in the archive or index.
